**Discipline Over Motivation: A Personal Reflection**

This article is a follow-up to a piece I wrote a while back about the idea of “One Day… or Day One.” The feedback I received back then was thoughtful, energising, and motivating, but the more I’ve reflected on it, the more I’ve realised that while motivation might spark the idea, it’s discipline that keeps the flame alive.

Over the past year, especially after picking up a leg injury at the tail end of last year, I’ve learned that my sense of discipline often outweighs my sense of motivation. It has its pros and cons, but it’s also one of the most powerful tools I’ve leaned on, particularly when motivation fades.

When I was finally able to run again, I set myself a challenge for the summer: not a full marathon, but an ultra marathon. It felt ambitious, maybe even a little ridiculous given the recovery I was coming out of, but it also felt like the right kind of test. The early weeks were slow and cautious, rebuilding strength and consistency.

Motivation helped me get moving, the routine kind of set in, but it was still difficult.  I had lost a lot of the strength and more over lost even more confidence given the injury I was recovering from.  **Motivation, as helpful as it was, was not reliable**.

The first real test came one morning when I wanted to sleep in. Just fifteen extra minutes. The excuses arrived quickly: “I deserve this,” “I’ve been running nearly every day,” “One morning won’t matter.” But discipline stepped in. “Right… we’re up in 5… 4… 3… 2… 1.”

I knew that missing my slot, even by a few minutes, would throw off my rhythm. So I got up. Out the door before six, back by seven, breakfast, shower, and straight into the first meeting of the day.

We’ve all been there. And that’s the point: **motivation doesn’t always show up** when it’s pouring rain at 5:45am, when you’d love a glass of wine on Saturday night but know it’ll spike your heart rate the next morning, when you’re tired, sore, recovering from injury, and no one’s watching.

> Motivation sets the ball bouncing, but without discipline, it dribbles off course. Motivation gives the ball potential energy; discipline converts it into kinetic energy: momentum, cadence, consistency.

**Discipline isn’t glamorous**. It doesn’t always feel good in the moment. But it builds the habits, routines, and resilience that carry us forward long after motivation fades.

Success, growth, relationships, they don’t happen just through motivation. **They happen through discipline.** It’s choosing to study instead of Netflix and chill. It’s lacing up your shoes instead of scrolling for five more minutes. It’s committing to yourself: being accountable, responsible, consistent.

Over time, I’ve come to see discipline not just as a tool, but as a form of self-respect. 

> It’s how we honour our goals, our values, and our potential.
