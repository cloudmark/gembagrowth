One of the easiest traps for managers to fall into is becoming the team's chief problem solver.

A machine stops running.

A customer escalates an issue.

A process breaks down.

An employee faces a challenge.

The manager steps in, provides the answer, and moves on to the next problem.

Initially, this feels like effective leadership. Problems are resolved quickly, decisions are made, and progress continues.

Over time, however, a hidden issue emerges.

The team becomes dependent on the manager.

Every challenge requires escalation.

Every decision flows upwards.

Every problem waits for someone else to fix it.

The organisation develops a culture of dependence instead of ownership.

Chris Voss addresses this challenge through the use of **calibrated questions**. Rather than telling people what to do, calibrated questions encourage them to think, engage, and participate in solving the problem themselves. While Voss developed this approach for negotiation, it translates exceptionally well into leadership and management.[ ](https://gsi-my.sharepoint.com/personal/matthew_galea_novanta_com/_layouts/15/Doc.aspx?sourcedoc=%7B69BFD05C-F718-4D80-AA0D-9BAC543B8D0D%7D&file=Never%20Split%20the%20Difference.pptx&action=edit&mobileredirect=true)[[Never Spli...Difference | PowerPoint]](https://gsi-my.sharepoint.com/personal/matthew_galea_novanta_com/_layouts/15/Doc.aspx?sourcedoc=%7B69BFD05C-F718-4D80-AA0D-9BAC543B8D0D%7D&file=Never%20Split%20the%20Difference.pptx&action=edit&mobileredirect=true)

The best managers often have the answers.

The smartest managers know when not to give them.

### **The Difference Between Solving and Leading**

Many supervisors are promoted because they are technically excellent.

They understand the process.

They know the equipment.

They have years of experience.

Naturally, when someone presents a problem, their instinct is to offer a solution.

The challenge is that every solution provided by a manager is a learning opportunity removed from the team.

Consider two different approaches.

#### **Manager One**

Employee:

*"We're struggling to hit today's production target."*

Manager:

*"Move Operator A to Station 3 and reschedule the final run."*

Problem solved.

#### **Manager Two**

Employee:

*"We're struggling to hit today's production target."*

Manager:

*"What options have you considered?"*

The conversation feels slower.

However, something valuable is happening.

The employee is learning to think through the challenge independently.

Over time, those small moments create stronger problem-solvers and more capable teams.

### **Why Questions Create Better Decisions**

One of the most important lessons from *Never Split the Difference* is that people support what they help create. Calibrated questions create involvement rather than compliance.[ ](https://gsi-my.sharepoint.com/personal/matthew_galea_novanta_com/_layouts/15/Doc.aspx?sourcedoc=%7B69BFD05C-F718-4D80-AA0D-9BAC543B8D0D%7D&file=Never%20Split%20the%20Difference.pptx&action=edit&mobileredirect=true)[[Never Spli...Difference | PowerPoint]](https://gsi-my.sharepoint.com/personal/matthew_galea_novanta_com/_layouts/15/Doc.aspx?sourcedoc=%7B69BFD05C-F718-4D80-AA0D-9BAC543B8D0D%7D&file=Never%20Split%20the%20Difference.pptx&action=edit&mobileredirect=true)

When managers constantly provide answers, employees may follow instructions.

When employees contribute to solutions, they develop ownership.

Ownership matters because people work harder to implement ideas they helped create.

Consider a continuous improvement project.

If management dictates every aspect of the solution, employees often see the initiative as management's responsibility.

If employees help identify the root causes and develop the improvement actions, the project becomes their responsibility as well.

The technical solution may be identical.

The level of commitment is not.

### **A Manufacturing Example**

I once worked with a supervisor who seemed to have all the answers.

He was knowledgeable, experienced, and highly respected.

Unfortunately, he was also overwhelmed.

Every issue came directly to him.

What made the situation interesting was that his team was highly capable.

The problem wasn't skill.

The problem was habit.

Employees had learned that the quickest route to a solution was asking the supervisor.

Eventually he made a deliberate change.

Whenever team members brought him a problem, he began responding with questions:

*"What do you think is causing it?"*

*"What have you already tried?"*

*"What options do we have?"*

*"What would happen if we did nothing?"*

Initially the team found it frustrating.

They wanted answers.

Within a few months, however, something remarkable happened.

The number of issues escalated to the supervisor decreased significantly.

The quality of decisions improved.

Employees became more confident.

The supervisor found himself spending less time firefighting and more time developing the team.

Nothing about the process changed.

The questions changed.

### **The Power of Calibrated Questions**

Voss recommends using open-ended questions that begin with words such as:

- What
- How
- When
- Where
- Who

These questions naturally encourage discussion and thinking.[ ](https://gsi-my.sharepoint.com/personal/matthew_galea_novanta_com/_layouts/15/Doc.aspx?sourcedoc=%7B69BFD05C-F718-4D80-AA0D-9BAC543B8D0D%7D&file=Never%20Split%20the%20Difference.pptx&action=edit&mobileredirect=true)[[Never Spli...Difference | PowerPoint]](https://gsi-my.sharepoint.com/personal/matthew_galea_novanta_com/_layouts/15/Doc.aspx?sourcedoc=%7B69BFD05C-F718-4D80-AA0D-9BAC543B8D0D%7D&file=Never%20Split%20the%20Difference.pptx&action=edit&mobileredirect=true)

Consider the difference between these two approaches:

#### **Closed Question**

*"Can you finish this by Friday?"*

The answer is either yes or no.

#### **Calibrated Question**

*"How can we ensure this is completed by Friday?"*

Now the individual must think about resources, obstacles, priorities, and solutions.

The quality of conversation immediately improves.

The manager receives more information.

The employee takes greater ownership.

Everyone benefits.

### **Using Questions During Change**

Change initiatives frequently fail because employees feel decisions have been imposed upon them.

Leaders spend considerable time explaining what is happening but relatively little time exploring what people think about it.

Questions can dramatically improve engagement.

For example:

- What concerns you most about this change?
- What risks should we be considering?
- What would help make the transition easier?
- How might this impact your daily work?

These questions achieve two objectives.

First, they reveal valuable information.

Second, they demonstrate respect.

People are far more likely to support change when they believe their perspective matters.

### **Performance Management and Coaching**

Questions are equally powerful during performance discussions.

Imagine an employee consistently missing deadlines.

Many managers begin with instruction.

*"You need to improve your planning."*

A coaching-oriented leader might ask:

*"What do you think is preventing deadlines from being met?"*

Followed by:

*"What changes could help?"*

*"What support would be useful?"*

*"How will you know things are improving?"*

Instead of creating dependence, the manager creates ownership.

The employee becomes an active participant in solving the problem rather than a passive recipient of feedback.

### **The Most Powerful Question in Leadership**

Of all the calibrated questions Voss discusses, one stands out because of its ability to shift responsibility back to the other person:

**"How am I supposed to do that?"**[ ](https://gsi-my.sharepoint.com/personal/matthew_galea_novanta_com/_layouts/15/Doc.aspx?sourcedoc=%7B69BFD05C-F718-4D80-AA0D-9BAC543B8D0D%7D&file=Never%20Split%20the%20Difference.pptx&action=edit&mobileredirect=true)[[Never Spli...Difference | PowerPoint]](https://gsi-my.sharepoint.com/personal/matthew_galea_novanta_com/_layouts/15/Doc.aspx?sourcedoc=%7B69BFD05C-F718-4D80-AA0D-9BAC543B8D0D%7D&file=Never%20Split%20the%20Difference.pptx&action=edit&mobileredirect=true)

In a management context, this question can uncover unrealistic expectations and stimulate problem-solving.

Imagine a customer demands an impossible delivery date.

Rather than immediately rejecting the request, a leader might ask:

*"How would you suggest we achieve that within the available capacity?"*

The discussion instantly becomes more collaborative.

People begin considering constraints, resources, trade-offs, and practical realities.

A question succeeds where an argument often fails.

### **Building a Problem-Solving Culture**

Continuous improvement depends on problem-solving.

Problem-solving depends on thinking.

Thinking depends on questions.

Many organisations invest heavily in systems, technology, and processes while overlooking one of the simplest leadership tools available.

Curiosity.

Leaders who ask better questions create teams that think more deeply, solve problems more effectively, and require less supervision.

Over time, this creates a culture where ownership grows naturally.

People stop waiting for instructions and start contributing solutions.

### **Practical Questions Every Leader Should Use**

The next time someone brings you a problem, try asking:

- What do you think is causing it?
- What options do we have?
- What have we not considered?
- What would success look like?
- What's the biggest obstacle?
- How can we solve this together?
- What would you recommend?

These questions are simple.

Their impact can be significant.

### **Final Thought**

Many managers believe leadership is about having the right answers.

Chris Voss offers a different perspective. The most influential people are often those who ask the best questions. Calibrated questions encourage ownership, create engagement, and unlock information that might otherwise remain hidden.[ ](https://gsi-my.sharepoint.com/personal/matthew_galea_novanta_com/_layouts/15/Doc.aspx?sourcedoc=%7B69BFD05C-F718-4D80-AA0D-9BAC543B8D0D%7D&file=Never%20Split%20the%20Difference.pptx&action=edit&mobileredirect=true)[[Never Spli...Difference | PowerPoint]](https://gsi-my.sharepoint.com/personal/matthew_galea_novanta_com/_layouts/15/Doc.aspx?sourcedoc=%7B69BFD05C-F718-4D80-AA0D-9BAC543B8D0D%7D&file=Never%20Split%20the%20Difference.pptx&action=edit&mobileredirect=true)

The next time someone brings you a problem, resist the temptation to provide an immediate solution.

Pause.

Get curious.

Ask a better question.

You may discover that the person in front of you already has more of the answer than you realised.
